Reduces the risk of failure: Building an MVP allows you to test your product idea in the real world with minimal investment. By validating your idea early on, you can identify any potential roadblocks and make changes before investing significant resources in a full product launch.

=== P ===
Saves time and money: An MVP is a pared-down version of your product that includes only the most essential features. By focusing on the core functionality, you can save time and money on development and testing.

=== P ===
Gets customer feedback: An MVP facilitates you to gather feedback from real customers who can provide valuable insights on what features are most important to them. This feedback can be used to refine your product and improve the user experience.

=== P ===
Iterative development: Building an MVP allows you to adopt an iterative development approach, where you can test and refine your product based on feedback and data. This means you can continuously improve your product and stay ahead of the competition.

=== P ===
Faster time to market: By building an MVP, you can get your product to market faster than if you were to develop a full product from scratch. This can help you stay ahead of the competition and capture market share early on.

=== P ===
Provides focus: Creating an MVP force you to prioritize the most important features of your product, as you only have limited resources to work with. This helps you stay focused on building the core value proposition of your product and avoid getting bogged down in unnecessary features or functionalities.
